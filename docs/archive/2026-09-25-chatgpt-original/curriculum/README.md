# Fit360 curriculum

Fit360 starts by defining its learning experience before implementing product features. The curriculum is the source of truth for learning goals, scope, safety language, sequencing, and future content requirements.

## Curriculum design

Each term should specify:

- Its intended learner outcomes and prerequisite knowledge.
- Modules and core vocabulary.
- Safe self-application activities that remain educational and non-clinical.
- Evidence-appraisal prompts and source expectations.
- Explicit safety boundaries and referral language where relevant.
- Assessment or recall activities that test understanding rather than clinical judgment.

## Terms

1. [Term 1 — The Body](term-01-the-body.md): anatomy, physiology, energy systems, and movement vocabulary.

Future terms may cover exercise science, nutrition, aging, evidence appraisal, and integrated safe self-application. Their order and scope should be added only after curriculum review.

## Content guardrails

Curriculum content supports medical literacy, not medical practice. It does not diagnose, prescribe treatment, provide rehabilitation, or offer therapeutic nutrition advice. When content touches symptoms, injury, medical conditions, medications, or therapeutic dietary needs, it must direct learners to qualified clinical support rather than attempt individualized guidance.

## Authoring workflow

1. Define learning outcomes and scope.
2. Identify authoritative, current sources and record evidence limits.
3. Draft plain-language learning material and safe examples.
4. Review technical accuracy, accessibility, and safety boundaries.
5. Add content only after the curriculum structure is approved; then map it to product requirements.
