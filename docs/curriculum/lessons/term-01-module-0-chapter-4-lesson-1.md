# Lesson 0.4.1 — How to use the lessons, videos and the app together

**Term 1, Module 0, Chapter 0.4, Lesson 1.** A complete, reviewable lesson outline for the fourth and final Module 0 lesson. Unlike Lessons 0.1.1–0.3.1, this chapter is product-content orientation, not health content.

## Chapter framing

**Why it matters:** every Fit360 lesson exists in three places — the app's lesson player, a YouTube video, and this curriculum's text. Learners who know why, and who know where a lesson's sources and recall cards actually live, get more out of all three and trust that everything traces back to the same place rather than to a commercial certification course.

**Learning objectives:**
1. Describe how one lesson exists as app content, a video and curriculum text, and why Fit360 builds it that way.
2. Locate the sources and recall cards for a given lesson.
3. State that Fit360's curriculum docs — not any certification course — are the canonical source the app and videos are built from.

**Learner capabilities (what a learner can do afterwards):**
- Given any Fit360 lesson, find its sources section and its recall cards.
- Explain in one sentence why the same lesson exists as text, video and in-app content, and which one is the source of truth if they ever seem to disagree.

**Prerequisites:** [0.1 — What Fit360 is and isn't](term-01-module-0-chapter-1-lesson-1.md).

**Keywords:** lesson player, recall cards, evidence card, canonical source.

**Concept map (textual; a visual diagram is a later design task, not built here):**
```
One lesson (for example 0.1.1)
   ├─ App: the lesson player (interactive, tracks progress)
   ├─ YouTube: the video version (public, shareable)
   └─ Curriculum docs (docs/curriculum/): the canonical text — source of truth
          ↓
   All three are built from, and trace back to, the same sources and the same safety boundary
```

**Safety and scope boundary:** this chapter is product orientation, not health content — nothing here changes what Fit360 can or cannot say (that remains Chapter 0.1's boundary). No self-application activity applies; there is nothing to observe about a learner's own body in this lesson.

**Diagnostic quiz (ungraded, before teaching, to surface assumptions):**
1. True or False: the YouTube video and the app lesson are allowed to say different things about safety from each other. *(False.)*
2. Multiple choice: if a fact in a video ever seemed to differ from the curriculum docs, which is treated as the source of truth? (a) the video, (b) the curriculum docs, (c) whichever was made first. *(b.)*
3. True or False: Fit360's lessons are based on a specific commercial personal-training certification's course material. *(False.)*

## Lesson body

**Question:** The same lesson exists as a video, an app lesson and a doc — why, and which one do I trust if they ever seem to disagree?

**Explanation:** Fit360 builds each lesson once, in the curriculum docs (`docs/curriculum/`), and that text is the canonical source — per [PROJECT_CONTEXT.md](../../PROJECT_CONTEXT.md#5-learning-architecture) and the [curriculum README](../README.md). The app's lesson player and the YouTube video are both built *from* that text, not the reverse, so if they ever seem to disagree, the curriculum doc wins and the difference gets fixed there first. Introduce two supporting ideas:
- **Recall cards** — short spaced-repetition prompts at the end of a lesson (see the recall cards in Lessons 0.1.1–0.3.1 for examples), used to reinforce what was just taught.
- **Sources / evidence cards** — where a lesson's factual claims trace back to, following Fit360's evidence hierarchy (taught in [Lesson 0.2.1](term-01-module-0-chapter-2-lesson-1.md)).

State plainly, per [AGENTS.md](../../../AGENTS.md): Fit360's curriculum is original writing. It is never copied from a certification body's course material, even though a commercial certification may occasionally serve as a benchmark.

**Visual/lab:** no interactive lab is attached to Module 0. Content requirement for design: a simple three-icon diagram (a book/text icon, a play button, a phone) all pointing toward one shared "source" icon.

**Self-check (ungraded, formative):** a short wayfinding check, not a knowledge test — for example:
- "Given a Fit360 lesson, where would you look to find where its facts come from? (a) the recall cards, (b) the sources section, (c) the diagnostic quiz." → (b).
- "If the app and a YouTube video ever disagreed, which one is treated as correct? (a) the video, (b) the app, (c) the curriculum doc they're both built from." → (c).

**Practical observation:** "Next time you watch a Fit360 video or open a lesson in the app, find one place where it points back to a source. Write down what kind of source it is (a guideline, a systematic review, a textbook, a professional organisation), using the evidence hierarchy from Lesson 0.2.1." This is a wayfinding task, not a health self-observation.

**Recall cards (spaced-repetition prompts):**
- Where does a lesson's list of sources live?
- What are recall cards for?
- Which is Fit360's canonical source: the app, the video, or the curriculum docs?

**Sources:** this lesson describes Fit360's own content architecture, approved by Pankaj in [PROJECT_CONTEXT.md](../../PROJECT_CONTEXT.md#5-learning-architecture) section 5 and the [curriculum README](../README.md)'s authoring workflow — not an external factual claim, so those two documents are cited directly and no further evidence citation is needed.
