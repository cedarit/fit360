# Lesson 4.1.1 — Joint actions and planes of motion, applied

**Term 1, Module 4, Chapter 4.1, Lesson 1.** The first lesson of Module 4 (Movement Vocabulary and Introductory Biomechanics). Connects the plane vocabulary from Lesson [1.2.1](term-01-module-1-chapter-2-lesson-1.md) and the joint types from Lesson [1.3.1](term-01-module-1-chapter-3-lesson-1.md) to the specific named joint actions this module and the Lat Pulldown Lab both depend on.

## Chapter framing

**Why it matters:** Lesson 1.2.1 previewed that movements like "bending an elbow" have their own name — a joint action — and Lesson 1.3.1 gave learners the hinge and ball-and-socket joint types those actions happen at. This lesson delivers on both previews: it names the joint actions directly and reconnects each one to its plane of motion. Every later chapter in Module 4, and the Lat Pulldown Lab's own vocabulary, assume a learner already has these terms.

**Learning objectives:**
1. Name and describe, in plain language, the joint actions: flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, internal (medial) rotation, external (lateral) rotation, pronation and supination.
2. Connect each joint action to the plane of motion it typically occurs in (from Lesson 1.2.1).
3. Given a simple, everyday movement, name the joint action(s) involved, at an introductory level.

**Learner capabilities (what a learner can do afterwards):**
- Given a labelled diagram or short clip of a simple joint movement, name the joint action.
- Explain, in one sentence each, what flexion/extension and abduction/adduction do to the angle or position of a limb.
- State which plane each joint action pair is typically associated with.

**Prerequisites:** [Lesson 1.2.1 — Directional terms and planes of motion](term-01-module-1-chapter-2-lesson-1.md); [Lesson 1.3.1 — The skeleton, joints and connective tissue](term-01-module-1-chapter-3-lesson-1.md).

**Keywords:** flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, internal (medial) rotation, external (lateral) rotation, pronation, supination.

**Safety and scope boundary:** this chapter teaches vocabulary for naming movement; it never judges whether a movement, joint or range of motion is normal, abnormal, safe or unsafe for a specific person. Naming a joint action ("this is elbow flexion") is description, not assessment — that distinction is taught explicitly in Chapter 4.5 and traces back to Chapter [0.3.1](term-01-module-0-chapter-3-lesson-1.md)'s standing referral rule.

**Diagnostic quiz (ungraded, before teaching, to surface assumptions):**
1. True or False: "flexion" always means bending a joint to make it smaller, no matter which joint. *(Broadly true for limb joints as taught here — sets up the general rule before any joint-specific nuance.)*
2. Multiple choice: which pair of joint actions happens mainly in the frontal (coronal) plane? (a) flexion/extension, (b) abduction/adduction, (c) pronation/supination. *(b.)*
3. True or False: pronation and supination are movements of the shoulder joint. *(False — sets up that they are specifically forearm movements, distinct from shoulder rotation.)*

## Lesson body

**Question:** Lesson 1.2.1 said "bending an elbow" happens in the sagittal plane, and mentioned it also has its own name. What is that name, and what are the other joint actions I need to know?

**Explanation:** OpenStax, *Anatomy and Physiology 2e*, §9.5, "Types of Body Movements":

- **Flexion and extension** — "Flexion and extension are typically movements that take place within the sagittal plane and involve anterior or posterior movements of the neck, trunk, or limbs." For a limb joint: "flexion decreases the angle between the bones (bending of the joint), while extension increases the angle and straightens the joint." *(Directly answers the opening question: "bending an elbow" is elbow flexion.)*
- **Abduction and adduction** — "Abduction and adduction motions occur within the coronal plane and involve medial-lateral motions of the limbs, fingers, toes, or thumb." Specifically: "Abduction moves the limb laterally away from the midline of the body, while adduction is the opposing movement that brings the limb toward the body or across the midline."
- **Internal (medial) and external (lateral) rotation** — "Movement that brings the anterior surface of the limb toward the midline of the body is called medial (internal) rotation. Conversely, rotation of the limb so that the anterior surface moves away from the midline is lateral (external) rotation."
- **Pronation and supination** — these are specifically "movements of the forearm," distinct from shoulder rotation. In the supinated position, the two forearm bones (radius and ulna) are parallel; in the pronated position, they cross to form an "X-shape."

Reconnecting to Lesson 1.2.1's planes: flexion/extension pair with the sagittal plane; abduction/adduction pair with the frontal (coronal) plane, exactly as those lessons previewed. Internal/external rotation and pronation/supination are rotational movements around a joint's long axis rather than movements confined to one of the three cardinal planes, and this lesson does not force them into a plane label that OpenStax's own definition does not use.

**Visual/lab:** no interactive lab is attached to Chapter 4.1 itself — this vocabulary is directly reused by the Lat Pulldown Lab (Chapter 4.6). Content requirement for design: short looping clips of each joint action pair (for example, elbow flexion/extension; shoulder abduction/adduction; forearm pronation/supination), each labelled with its plane where applicable, matching the format used for Lesson 1.2.1's plane clips.

**Self-check (ungraded, formative):**
- Labelling: given a short clip or diagram of a simple joint movement, name the joint action.
- Matching: match each joint action pair to the plane it typically occurs in (or "rotational, no single plane" for internal/external rotation and pronation/supination).

**Practical observation (safe self-application):** "Perform (or picture) one simple, pain-free arm or leg movement. Name the joint action using today's vocabulary, and say which plane it's associated with if it has one." This is naming a joint action, not assessing whether the movement is correct, safe or healthy — no form or injury-risk judgement is made, consistent with the safe self-application rule set in Chapter 0.3.

**Recall cards (spaced-repetition prompts):**
- What does flexion do to the angle at a joint? What does extension do?
- Which plane are abduction and adduction associated with?
- Which two joint actions are specific to the forearm, rather than the whole limb?

This lesson makes no population-specific claim, so no Indian-population evidence caveat applies.

## Explicitly out of scope for this lesson

- No claim about an individual's flexibility, range of motion, joint health or movement quality.
- No mention of circumduction, opposition, elevation/depression, inversion/eversion, dorsiflexion or plantar flexion — not required by this module's current scope; may be added later if a specific exercise needs them.
- No force, torque or leverage content — Chapter 4.2's scope.
- No evaluative language ("safe," "unsafe," "correct," "incorrect") applied to any movement — Chapter 4.5's explicit scope.
