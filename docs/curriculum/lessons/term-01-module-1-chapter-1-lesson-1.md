# Lesson 1.1.1 — Anatomical position, body regions and organ systems

**Term 1, Module 1, Chapter 1.1, Lesson 1.** The first lesson of Module 1, and Term 1's first lesson to make real factual anatomical claims — Module 0's lessons cited only Fit360's own policy documents. This lesson introduces the shared "map" every later Term 1 chapter, and the Lat Pulldown Lab, assumes learners already have.

## Chapter framing

**Why it matters:** every later description of movement in this curriculum — a joint action, a muscle's role, the Lat Pulldown Lab's toggles — depends on a shared reference frame. Without a standard body "map," terms like "front," "back," "near the trunk" and "far from the trunk" mean something different for every learner. This lesson builds that shared map before any of it is used.

**Learning objectives:**
1. Describe the anatomical position and explain why it's used as a fixed reference, regardless of how a person is actually standing or moving at any given moment.
2. Name the major body regions using the axial/appendicular distinction, and give an example of each.
3. List the organ systems most relevant to movement and exercise (musculoskeletal, nervous, cardiovascular, respiratory, endocrine) and state one movement-relevant role for each.

**Learner capabilities (what a learner can do afterwards):**
- Picture (or stand in) the anatomical position and use it as the reference point when describing where something is on the body.
- Point to an example of an axial region and an appendicular region on their own body.
- Name one thing each of the five movement-relevant organ systems does during exercise.

**Prerequisites:** Module 0 complete (Lessons [0.1.1](term-01-module-0-chapter-1-lesson-1.md)–0.4.1).

**Keywords:** anatomical position, superior/inferior, proximal/distal, axial/appendicular, organ system.

**Concept map (textual; a visual diagram is a later design task, not built here):**
```
Anatomical position (the fixed reference "map")
   │
   ├─ Body regions
   │     ├─ Axial — head, neck and trunk, along the body's central vertical axis
   │     └─ Appendicular — the limbs, and the girdles attaching them to the trunk
   │
   └─ Organ systems relevant to movement
         ├─ Musculoskeletal — supports the body and enables movement
         ├─ Nervous — senses the body and activates the responses that produce movement
         ├─ Cardiovascular — delivers oxygen and nutrients to tissue
         ├─ Respiratory — removes carbon dioxide and delivers oxygen to the blood
         └─ Endocrine — secretes hormones that regulate the body's processes
```

**Safety and scope boundary:** this chapter names and locates structures; it never evaluates anyone's body. A learner pointing to "my appendicular region" is practising vocabulary, not checking anything about their health, posture or structure. The standard Module 0 boundary applies throughout (see [Lesson 0.1.1](term-01-module-0-chapter-1-lesson-1.md) and [0.3.1](term-01-module-0-chapter-3-lesson-1.md)).

**Diagnostic quiz (ungraded, before teaching, to surface assumptions):**
1. True or False: the anatomical position describes however you happen to be standing right now. *(False — it's a fixed reference, independent of actual posture.)*
2. Multiple choice: which of these is an appendicular structure? (a) the skull, (b) the humerus (upper arm bone), (c) the vertebral column. *(b.)*
3. True or False: only the muscles are involved in movement — the nervous system plays no role. *(False.)*

## Lesson body

**Question:** Before I can describe any movement precisely, I need a shared "map" of the body. What is that map, and what are its main parts?

**Explanation:**
- **Anatomical position:** the body standing upright, feet at shoulder width with toes pointing forward, upper limbs at the sides with palms facing forward (OpenStax, *Anatomy and Physiology 2e*, §1.6, "Anatomical Terminology"). It's used as a fixed reference so that directional terms mean the same thing for every body, in every actual posture — a person lying down or mid-movement is still described *as if* in the anatomical position.
- **Body regions — axial and appendicular** (OpenStax, §7.1, "Divisions of the Skeletal System"): the **axial** skeleton/region is the head, neck and trunk, along the body's central vertical axis; the **appendicular** skeleton/region is the limbs, plus the girdles (shoulder and hip) that attach them to the trunk. This distinction is introduced in OpenStax's skeletal-system chapter rather than its terminology chapter; this lesson borrows it as a general way to talk about body regions (not bones specifically), a common and widely used simplification in introductory anatomy teaching.
- **A preview of superior/inferior and proximal/distal:** *superior* means toward the head, *inferior* toward the feet, in the anatomical position; *proximal* means closer to the trunk along a limb, *distal* means farther from it. These are named here so the vocabulary isn't new later, but full teaching and practice is Lesson 1.2.1 (Chapter 1.2, not yet written).
- **Organ systems relevant to movement** (OpenStax, §1.2, "Structural Organization of the Human Body"): of the eleven organ systems OpenStax describes there (integumentary, skeletal, muscular, nervous, endocrine, cardiovascular, lymphatic, respiratory, digestive, urinary and reproductive), Term 1 focuses on the five most directly relevant to movement and exercise. Their movement-relevant roles below closely follow that section's own wording: the **skeletal** and **muscular** systems — grouped here as **musculoskeletal** — "support the body and enable movement" and also help maintain body temperature; the **nervous** system "detects and processes sensory information and activates bodily responses"; the **cardiovascular** system "delivers oxygen and nutrients to the tissues"; the **respiratory** system "removes carbon dioxide from the body and delivers oxygen to the blood"; and the **endocrine** system "secretes hormones and regulates bodily processes." Module 2 unpacks each of these one at a time, with its own sourcing as those lessons are drafted.

**Visual/lab:** no interactive lab is attached to Chapter 1.1 (the flagship lab is later, in Module 4). Content requirement for design: a labelled body outline in the anatomical position, with the axial region shaded one colour and the appendicular region another; a separate row of five icons for the five movement-relevant organ systems.

**Self-check (ungraded, formative):**
- Label 4–5 points on a body outline as axial or appendicular.
- Match each of the five organ systems to one movement-relevant role (for example, "delivers oxygen to working muscle" → cardiovascular).

**Practical observation (safe self-application):** "Stand up (or picture yourself) in the anatomical position. Touch one axial-region body part and one appendicular-region body part, and say out loud which is which." This is naming and locating vocabulary, not assessing anything about the learner's body.

**Recall cards (spaced-repetition prompts):**
- What is the anatomical position, and why is it used as a fixed reference?
- Name the two body-region categories and give one example of each.
- Name the five organ systems most relevant to movement, and one role for each.

**Sources:** OpenStax, *Anatomy and Physiology 2e* — three sections, each cited for a specific claim:
- §1.6, ["Anatomical Terminology"](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/1-6-anatomical-terminology) — the anatomical position definition.
- §7.1, ["Divisions of the Skeletal System"](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/7-1-divisions-of-the-skeletal-system) — the axial/appendicular distinction.
- §1.2, ["Structural Organization of the Human Body"](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/1-2-structural-organization-of-the-human-body) — the organ-system list and each system's stated role.

**Source limitations (recorded per the [evidence policy](../../PROJECT_CONTEXT.md#4-evidence-policy)):** OpenStax's §1.2 lists eleven organ systems separately, including skeletal and muscular as two distinct systems. This lesson groups them together as "musculoskeletal" for a movement-focused overview, matching Term 1's own learning outcomes in [term-01-the-body.md](../term-01-the-body.md) — that grouping is a Fit360 framing choice for teaching purposes, not a claim made by the source itself. The axial/appendicular distinction is drawn from a chapter about the skeleton specifically (§7.1); this lesson extends it to describe body regions generally, which is a common introductory-teaching simplification, not a claim OpenStax itself makes about non-skeletal structures. Anatomical terminology of this kind is not population-dependent, so no Indian-population evidence caveat applies to this particular lesson (unlike some later Term 1 content, for example energy-system duration ranges).
