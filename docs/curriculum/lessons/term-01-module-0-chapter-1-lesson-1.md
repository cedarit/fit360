# Lesson 0.1.1 — What Fit360 is and isn't

**Term 1, Module 0, Chapter 0.1, Lesson 1.** This is Fit360's first vertical lesson slice: a complete, reviewable lesson outline, written to demonstrate the full lesson pattern end to end.

**Status:** Pankaj has confirmed Module 0 as the anchor for Fit360's first vertical lesson slice — this lesson is that slice. See [DECISIONS.md](../../DECISIONS.md#decided).

## Chapter framing

**Why it matters:** learners are about to spend months building health and fitness knowledge with Fit360, including help from an AI assistant later on. If they don't know what kind of help this is, they may reasonably expect diagnosis or treatment advice Fit360 will never give, or may distrust the product when it declines to answer a medical question. This lesson sets the right expectation before any other content.

**Learning objectives:**
1. State the difference between medical literacy and medical practice in plain language.
2. Identify at least three things Fit360 will never do, and explain why each boundary exists.
3. Recognise the standing referral line and state when it applies.

**Learner capabilities (what a learner can do afterwards):**
- Explain to someone else why Fit360 won't say whether their blood test result is normal.
- Notice when a lesson, video or (later) the AI assistant should be pointing them to a doctor instead of teaching them.

**Prerequisites:** none. This is the entry lesson for the entire curriculum.

**Keywords:** medical literacy, medical practice, diagnosis, treatment, rehabilitation, therapeutic diet, standing referral line.

**Concept map (textual; a visual diagram is a later design task, not built here):**
```
Fit360 (medical literacy)
  ├─ contrasts with → Medical practice
  │                     ├─ diagnosis
  │                     ├─ treatment / medication
  │                     ├─ rehabilitation
  │                     └─ therapeutic diet
  ├─ always includes → Standing referral line
  └─ feeds into → every later Term and lesson
```

**Safety and scope boundary (quoted from [safety-and-ai-policy.md](../../product/safety-and-ai-policy.md), the source of authority for this lesson):**
- What Fit360 may do: explain how the body works and the general science of training, nutrition, body composition, ageing and lifestyle disease; explain what a health test measures and what guidelines generally say, with sources; summarise research in plain language, including its strength and limits; give general training guidance to healthy adults with the reasoning shown; help a learner write good questions for their doctor.
- What Fit360 must never do: diagnose a condition or suggest someone probably has or doesn't have one; interpret a person's own test results as normal/abnormal or a risk verdict; prescribe or adjust treatment, medication or supplement doses for a medical purpose; provide rehabilitation or injury-management programmes; give therapeutic-diet advice; encourage training through pain or concerning symptoms.
- Standing referral line: *"Stop and speak to a qualified doctor or clinician. Fit360 is education, not medical assessment or treatment."*

**Diagnostic quiz (ungraded, before teaching, to surface assumptions):**
1. True or False: Fit360's AI assistant can tell you whether your cholesterol test result is normal. *(False — sets up the lesson.)*
2. True or False: Fit360 can explain what a cholesterol test generally measures. *(True.)*
3. Multiple choice: If a lesson activity asks you to notice your own breathing during a walk, is that (a) an observation exercise, (b) a medical test, (c) a diagnosis? *(a)*

## Lesson body

**Question:** If Fit360 isn't a doctor, what exactly is it — and what won't it do for me?

**Explanation:** Fit360 provides *medical literacy* — understanding how the body works, what the evidence says, and how to think about health decisions — not *medical practice*, which requires a licensed clinician who knows an individual's full history. Walk through each "must never" boundary from the safety policy with a one-line reason:
- No diagnosis, because diagnosis needs clinical training, examination and context Fit360 cannot have or ethically claim to have.
- No interpretation of an individual's test results, for the same reason — a number without clinical context can mislead rather than inform.
- No treatment, medication or supplement dosing advice, because that is a clinical act with individual risk that only a qualified professional can weigh.
- No rehabilitation, because injury recovery needs individualised, supervised assessment.
- No therapeutic-diet advice, because treating a condition through diet is a clinical intervention, not general nutrition education.

Close with the standing referral line, presented as a feature, not a disclaimer: it is how learners know Fit360 is being honest about what it can and can't do for them.

**Visual/lab:** no interactive lab is attached to Module 0. Content requirement for design: a simple two-column "Fit360 can / Fit360 cannot" card list, illustrated with plain iconography (books, magnifying glass, a lesson player) — explicitly no stock medical imagery (stethoscopes, clinical settings, test tubes) that would imply diagnosis or treatment is on offer.

**Self-check (ungraded, formative):** "Spot the boundary" — sort 4–6 short statements into "Fit360 can say this" / "Fit360 must not say this," for example:
- "This test generally measures X." → can say.
- "Your result means you're at risk of Y." → must not say.
- "Here are general questions to ask your doctor about X." → can say.
- "Take this supplement to treat Y." → must not say.

Each answer shows a one-line explanation immediately after the learner responds.

**Practical observation (non-clinical, everyday-life task — matches the safe self-application rule in Chapter 0.3):** "Next time you see a health claim or advertisement — for a supplement, a fitness gadget, a diet plan — notice whether it's teaching you something general or telling you something specific about your own body. Write down one example." This asks learners to observe the world around them, not their own health status.

**Recall cards (spaced-repetition prompts):**
- What is the standing referral line?
- Name two things Fit360 will never do, and why.
- What is the difference between medical literacy and medical practice?

**Sources:** this lesson teaches Fit360's own scope decision, approved by Pankaj, rather than an external factual claim about the body — so its source is [safety-and-ai-policy.md](../../product/safety-and-ai-policy.md) itself, cited directly above. No external evidence citation is required for this lesson. Later chapters that make factual anatomical, physiological or biomechanical claims (starting at Module 1) must cite per the evidence policy in [PROJECT_CONTEXT.md](../../PROJECT_CONTEXT.md#4-evidence-policy), and record any source limitations at that time.

## Explicitly out of scope for this lesson

- No anatomy, physiology or biomechanics content — that starts in Module 1.
- No specific claims about any supplement, test or diet, even as examples — the "spot the boundary" statements above are generic templates, not real product claims.
- No discussion of the AI assistant's implementation — only the behavioural rule it must follow, which this lesson also teaches by extension.
