# Fit360

**Learn · train · understand**

Fit360 is a personal, university-style web app for learning the science that supports safe, thoughtful fitness practice. It begins with a structured curriculum and later connects learning to personal training context.

## Purpose

Fit360 teaches foundational anatomy, physiology, biomechanics, exercise science, nutrition, aging, evidence appraisal, and safe self-application. Its aim is to help learners understand *why* training and health guidance may work, evaluate claims responsibly, and make sensible non-clinical decisions.

## Safety boundary

Fit360 provides medical literacy, not medical practice. It must never diagnose a condition, prescribe treatment, provide rehabilitation, or give therapeutic nutrition advice. Content and product experiences must:

- Use educational, non-diagnostic language.
- Encourage users to seek an appropriately qualified clinician for symptoms, injury, illness, medication questions, or individualized therapeutic guidance.
- Clearly distinguish general education from individualized clinical care.
- Avoid collecting or inferring information for diagnosis or treatment.

## Product principles

- **Curriculum first:** establish a coherent, reviewable learning structure before building app features.
- **Evidence-aware:** identify the strength, limits, and applicability of claims rather than presenting certainty where it does not exist.
- **Practical but bounded:** translate concepts into safe general self-application without crossing into clinical advice.
- **Plain language:** teach technical ideas precisely while making them approachable.
- **Progressive learning:** move from vocabulary and core systems to integrated application and evidence appraisal.

## Delivery workflow

Development uses Git, Jira, pull requests, and test-driven development. Changes are covered as appropriate by unit, integration, and UI tests, with Playwright for end-to-end/UI coverage. Supported environments are local development and production only.

See [the delivery lifecycle](engineering/delivery-lifecycle.md) for the operating model and [the curriculum](curriculum/README.md) for the curriculum-first starting point.
