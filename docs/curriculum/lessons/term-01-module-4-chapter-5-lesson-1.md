# Lesson 4.5.1 — Describing movement without labelling it normal, abnormal, safe or unsafe

**Term 1, Module 4, Chapter 4.5, Lesson 1.** The last content chapter of Module 4 before the flagship lab (Chapter 4.6). Sets the safety-language rule that the Lat Pulldown Lab and all of Term 2 must follow, using the descriptive vocabulary built in Lessons [4.1.1](term-01-module-4-chapter-1-lesson-1.md)–[4.4.1](term-01-module-4-chapter-4-lesson-1.md).

## Chapter framing

**Why it matters:** Module 4 has given learners real vocabulary for describing movement — joint actions, force and torque, stability, mobility and flexibility. It would be easy, and dangerous, to slide from *describing* a movement into *judging* it: "that squat looks unsafe," "your form is wrong." Fit360 never does this for an individual, for the same reason it never interprets an individual's test result (see the [safety and AI policy](../../product/safety-and-ai-policy.md)) — that judgement requires seeing the whole person, and belongs to a qualified professional, not a curriculum or an app. This chapter is itself the safety-boundary chapter for everything Module 4 has built.

**Learning objectives:**
1. Explain why Fit360 avoids labelling an individual's movement as normal, abnormal, safe or unsafe.
2. Practise describing a movement using only Lessons 4.1.1–4.4.1's vocabulary, rather than a value judgement.
3. Restate the standing referral rule in this specific context.

**Learner capabilities (what a learner can do afterwards):**
- State, in one or two sentences, why Fit360 doesn't label an individual's movement as safe, unsafe, normal or abnormal.
- Rewrite an evaluative statement about a movement ("that looks unsafe") into a descriptive one, using Module 4's own vocabulary.
- State what a learner should do if they have a genuine concern about their own movement, pain or injury risk.

**Prerequisites:** [Lesson 4.1.1](term-01-module-4-chapter-1-lesson-1.md), [Lesson 4.2.1](term-01-module-4-chapter-2-lesson-1.md), [Lesson 4.3.1](term-01-module-4-chapter-3-lesson-1.md), [Lesson 4.4.1](term-01-module-4-chapter-4-lesson-1.md) (their vocabulary is reused throughout); [Lesson 0.3.1](term-01-module-0-chapter-3-lesson-1.md) (the standing referral rule this chapter restates).

**Safety and scope boundary:** this chapter *is* the safety boundary for Module 4's vocabulary. It never labels any specific person's movement, technique or form as good, bad, correct, incorrect, safe or unsafe — that is a movement assessment, which requires seeing an individual directly and is outside what a curriculum, video or app can responsibly do. This mirrors the same boundary already set for test results and health data in the [safety and AI policy](../../product/safety-and-ai-policy.md): Fit360 explains general concepts, never verdicts on an individual.

## Lesson body

**Question:** Now that I know all this vocabulary, can Fit360 tell me whether my own movement looks okay?

**Explanation:** No — and this chapter explains why, using Fit360's own stated scope.

- **Why Fit360 avoids evaluative movement language:** the [safety and AI policy](../../product/safety-and-ai-policy.md) already establishes that Fit360's AI assistant must not say whether an individual's test values are "good, bad, normal or risky" — it may only explain general concepts and direct the person to a qualified clinician. The same reasoning applies to movement: judging whether a specific person's technique is safe or correct requires seeing that person directly — their full context, history and goals — which is exactly the kind of individual judgement Fit360's medical-literacy-not-medical-practice boundary (Lesson [0.1.1](term-01-module-0-chapter-1-lesson-1.md)) rules out.

- **Descriptive vs. evaluative language, using Module 4's own vocabulary:** a descriptive statement uses Lessons 4.1.1–4.4.1's terms to say *what* is happening — "the knee flexes to about a right angle," "the feet are close together, giving a narrower base of support," "there's more forward lean than in the previous rep." An evaluative statement makes a judgement about whether that's good, bad, safe, unsafe, correct or incorrect for *this specific person*. Fit360 teaches and uses only the first kind.

- **Restating the standing referral rule in this context:** if a learner has a genuine concern about their own movement, technique, pain or injury risk, the answer is the same standing referral rule taught in Lesson 0.3.1: *"Stop and speak to a qualified doctor or clinician. Fit360 is education, not medical assessment or treatment."* For movement and technique specifically, this can also mean a qualified coach or trainer who can assess the learner directly — but the key point is the same: this is not something a curriculum, video or app can safely do instead.

## Explicitly out of scope for this lesson

- No movement assessment, technique correction or form judgement for any individual — that is precisely what this chapter exists to rule out.
- No new safety-policy content — this lesson restates the existing standing referral rule and safety-and-ai-policy.md boundary; it does not modify either.
- No claim that any specific exercise, stance or technique is inherently safe or unsafe in general — Fit360 avoids that framing entirely, not only for individuals.
