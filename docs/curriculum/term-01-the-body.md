# Term 1 — The Body

Term 1 builds the shared language for everything that follows in Fit360. It introduces the body as a set of interacting systems. Learners come away able to describe movement and energy use precisely, without making medical judgements.

**Status:** structure drafted; awaiting Pankaj's approval. Lessons are not yet written.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the term, learners should be able to:

1. Explain how Fit360 treats evidence, and the difference between medical literacy and medical care.
2. Identify major body regions, the anatomical position, planes, directions, and commonly referenced bones, joints and muscles at an introductory level.
3. Explain in broad terms how the musculoskeletal, nervous, cardiovascular, respiratory and endocrine systems contribute to movement and exercise.
4. Describe how ATP is supplied by overlapping energy systems, relate them to different activity demands, and explain recovery as a general concept.
5. Use basic movement and biomechanics terms accurately: flexion, extension, rotation, abduction, adduction, force, torque, leverage, stability, mobility and range of motion.
6. Recognise the limits of self-observation, and know when symptoms, injury or illness need a qualified clinician.

## Modules

### Module 0 — Orientation: evidence, scope and safety

- What Fit360 is and isn't: medical literacy, not medical practice
- How to judge a claim: source types, the evidence hierarchy, and "how sure are we?"
- Safe self-application and the standing referral rule
- How to use the lessons, the videos and the app together

### Module 1 — Anatomy for Movement

- Anatomical position, body regions and major organ systems
- Directional terms and planes of motion
- Skeleton, joints and connective tissue
- Major muscles and how they are named

### Module 2 — Physiology of Movement

- Nervous system: sensation, coordination and motor control
- Muscle: structure, contraction and fibre types at an introductory level
- Cardiovascular and respiratory roles in oxygen transport and exchange
- Endocrine foundations and homeostasis; acute responses to activity

### Module 3 — Energy Systems and Recovery

- ATP as the immediate energy currency
- The phosphagen, glycolytic and oxidative pathways
- How the systems overlap, and the influence of duration and intensity
- Recovery as a general physiological concept

### Module 4 — Movement Vocabulary and Introductory Biomechanics

- Joint actions and planes of motion
- Force, torque, leverage, balance and centre of mass
- Mobility, stability, coordination and movement variability
- Describing movement without labelling it normal, abnormal, safe or unsafe for a specific person
- **Flagship lab:** the Lat Pulldown Lab (see [PROJECT_CONTEXT.md](../PROJECT_CONTEXT.md#first-interactive-lab))

## Safe self-application

Activities may ask learners to observe and describe their own movement in neutral language, for example by naming the plane of a simple motion or noticing changes in breathing during ordinary activity. They must not ask learners to self-diagnose, test through pain, continue through concerning symptoms or use lessons as rehabilitation.

Standing guidance: *stop activity and seek qualified care for concerning symptoms, injury or health questions. Fit360 education is not a substitute for medical assessment, treatment, rehabilitation or therapeutic nutrition advice.*

## Evidence and assessment

Learning checks focus on retrieval, matching terms, labelling diagrams, explaining how systems interact and interpreting general claims with evidence in mind. They never assess diagnosis, treatment selection, rehabilitation planning or individual nutrition therapy.
