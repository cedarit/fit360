# Term 1 — The Body

Term 1 establishes the shared language needed for later Fit360 study. It introduces the body as an interacting set of systems and gives learners a grounded vocabulary for discussing movement and exercise without making medical judgments.

## Scope

- Foundational anatomy
- Foundational physiology
- Energy systems
- Movement vocabulary

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the term, learners should be able to:

1. Identify major body regions, anatomical positions, planes, directions, and commonly referenced structures at an introductory level.
2. Explain, in broad terms, how musculoskeletal, cardiovascular, respiratory, and nervous systems contribute to movement and exercise.
3. Describe how ATP is supplied by overlapping energy systems and relate their broad roles to different activity demands.
4. Use basic movement terminology—such as flexion, extension, rotation, abduction, adduction, stability, mobility, force, and range of motion—accurately in general educational discussion.
5. Recognize the limits of self-observation and know when symptoms, injury, illness, or other concerns require qualified clinical support.

## Modules

### 1. Anatomical orientation

- Anatomical position, body regions, and major organ systems
- Directional terms and planes of motion
- Introductory skeletal, joint, and muscle terminology

### 2. Systems in motion

- Musculoskeletal roles in producing and transmitting force
- Cardiovascular and respiratory roles in oxygen transport and exchange
- Nervous-system roles in sensation, coordination, and motor control
- Homeostasis and acute responses to activity

### 3. Energy for activity

- ATP as the immediate energy currency
- Phosphagen, glycolytic, and oxidative energy pathways
- Overlap among systems and the influence of activity duration and intensity
- Recovery as a general physiological concept

### 4. Movement vocabulary

- Joint actions and planes of motion
- Force, torque, leverage, balance, and centre of mass at an introductory level
- Mobility, stability, coordination, and movement variability
- Describing movement without labeling it as normal, abnormal, safe, or unsafe for a specific person

## Safe self-application

Activities may ask learners to observe and describe their own movement using neutral language, for example identifying the plane of a simple motion or noting changes in breathing during ordinary activity. They must not ask learners to self-diagnose, test through pain, continue through concerning symptoms, or use lessons as rehabilitation.

Use this standing guidance: stop activity and seek qualified care for concerning symptoms, injury, or health questions. Fit360 education is not a substitute for medical assessment, treatment, rehabilitation, or therapeutic nutrition advice.

## Evidence and assessment

Learning checks should prioritize retrieval, terminology matching, diagram labeling, explanations of system interactions, and evidence-aware interpretation of general claims. They must not assess learners on diagnosis, treatment selection, rehabilitation planning, or individualized nutritional therapy.

## Completion criteria

Term 1 is complete when its outcomes, module-level objectives, safety review, evidence references, and assessment blueprint have been approved. Product features should then be built to serve this defined structure.
