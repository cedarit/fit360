# Term 1 — The Body

Term 1 builds the shared language for everything that follows in Fit360. It introduces the body as a set of interacting systems. Learners come away able to describe movement and energy use precisely, without making medical judgements.

**Status:** chapter-level architecture and the first vertical lesson slice drafted; awaiting Pankaj's approval. Only one lesson (below) and the Lat Pulldown Lab spec are written in full; the remaining chapters are outlines (outcomes, vocabulary, assessment and safety shape) until Term 1 itself is approved.

**Related deliverables:**
- First vertical lesson slice: [Lesson 0.1.1 — What Fit360 is and isn't](lessons/term-01-module-0-chapter-1-lesson-1.md)
- Flagship lab specification: [Lat Pulldown Lab](labs/lat-pulldown-lab.md)
- Open decision raised by this draft: see [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md#open), "First vertical lesson slice topic"

## Chapter numbering

Chapters are numbered `<module>.<chapter>` (for example `1.4` is Module 1, Chapter 4). Lessons within a chapter are numbered `<module>.<chapter>.<lesson>`. Only `0.1.1` exists as a full lesson today; every other chapter lists outcomes and shape only, not lesson-by-lesson content.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the term, learners should be able to:

1. Explain how Fit360 treats evidence, and the difference between medical literacy and medical care.
2. Identify major body regions, the anatomical position, planes, directions, and commonly referenced bones, joints and muscles at an introductory level.
3. Explain in broad terms how the musculoskeletal, nervous, cardiovascular, respiratory and endocrine systems contribute to movement and exercise.
4. Describe how ATP is supplied by overlapping energy systems, relate them to different activity demands, and explain recovery as a general concept.
5. Use basic movement and biomechanics terms accurately: flexion, extension, rotation, abduction, adduction, force, torque, leverage, stability, mobility and range of motion.
6. Recognise the limits of self-observation, and know when symptoms, injury or illness need a qualified clinician.

Every chapter below inherits Fit360's universal chapter pattern from [PROJECT_CONTEXT.md](../PROJECT_CONTEXT.md#5-learning-architecture): why it matters, learning objectives, learner capabilities, prerequisites, keywords, a concept map, the safety and scope boundary, and a short diagnostic quiz. Chapter tables below give the parts specific to Term 1; the shared safety boundary is [the standing referral rule](#safe-self-application) and applies to every chapter.

## Module 0 — Orientation: evidence, scope and safety

No prerequisites. This module must be completed before any other, because it defines the vocabulary and safety rules every later chapter relies on.

| # | Chapter | Prerequisites | Core vocabulary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.1 | What Fit360 is and isn't | None | medical literacy, medical practice, diagnosis, treatment, rehabilitation, therapeutic diet, standing referral line |
| 0.2 | How to judge a claim | 0.1 | evidence hierarchy, systematic review, meta-analysis, primary source, conflict of interest, population validity, certainty language |
| 0.3 | Safe self-application and the standing referral rule | 0.1 | self-observation, self-diagnosis, standing referral rule, concerning symptoms |
| 0.4 | How to use the lessons, videos and the app together | 0.1 | lesson player, recall cards, evidence card, canonical source |

**0.1 — What Fit360 is and isn't**
- Outcomes: state the medical-literacy/medical-practice boundary in plain language; name at least three things Fit360 will never do, and why each exists; recognise the standing referral line and when it applies.
- Assessment: ungraded "spot the boundary" exercise — sort short statements into "Fit360 can say this" / "Fit360 must not say this," with the reason shown after each answer.
- Safe self-application: none yet to apply; this chapter only sets the rule.
- YouTube mapping: strong first-video candidate — the channel/app's framing video. Full lesson outline drafted below and linked above.

**0.2 — How to judge a claim**
- Outcomes: rank source types by strength using the evidence hierarchy; explain why evidence from Indian and non-Indian populations isn't always interchangeable; assess one example claim for confidence and say why.
- Assessment: applied exercise — rank/critique 3–4 short, clearly fictional example claims with sources attached; formative, with model answers shown after submission.
- Safe self-application: learners may practise this skill on public claims they encounter elsewhere (for example a supplement advertisement); Fit360 never asks them to evaluate their own medical situation.
- YouTube mapping: strong second-video candidate; reusable as a "how to read health news" explainer, referenced again before Term 5.

**0.3 — Safe self-application and the standing referral rule**
- Outcomes: state the standing referral line from memory and explain why it exists; distinguish neutral self-observation from self-diagnosis; identify situations that require stopping and seeing a clinician rather than continuing a lesson activity.
- Assessment: scenario quiz — vignettes where the learner decides "continue the lesson activity" vs "stop and refer to a clinician," with explanations.
- Safe self-application: this chapter defines the boundary every later self-application activity in the curriculum inherits.
- YouTube mapping: short-form explainer, likely reused as a pinned clip on every video rather than a standalone long video.

**0.4 — How to use the lessons, videos and the app together**
- Outcomes: describe how one lesson exists as app content, a video and curriculum text, and why; locate sources and recall cards for a lesson; state that the app (not certification material) is Fit360's canonical source.
- Assessment: ungraded wayfinding check ("where would you find X"), not a knowledge test.
- Safe self-application: not applicable — product orientation, not health content.
- YouTube mapping: optional; may fold into channel trailer/about content instead of a standalone lesson video.
- Full lesson outline: [Lesson 0.4.1](lessons/term-01-module-0-chapter-4-lesson-1.md).

## Module 1 — Anatomy for Movement

Prerequisite: Module 0.

| # | Chapter | Prerequisites | Core vocabulary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.1 | Anatomical position, body regions and organ systems | Module 0 | anatomical position, superior/inferior, proximal/distal, axial/appendicular, organ system |
| 1.2 | Directional terms and planes of motion | 1.1 | sagittal, frontal (coronal) and transverse planes; anterior, posterior, medial, lateral, superior, inferior, proximal, distal |
| 1.3 | The skeleton, joints and connective tissue | 1.1, 1.2 | axial/appendicular skeleton, joint, ligament, tendon, cartilage, hinge joint, ball-and-socket joint |
| 1.4 | Major muscles and how they are named | 1.1–1.3 | prime mover, synergist, stabiliser, origin, insertion, muscle-naming conventions |

**1.1 — Anatomical position, body regions and organ systems**
- Outcomes: describe the anatomical position and why it's used as a reference; name the major body regions; list the organ systems relevant to movement and state one movement-relevant role for each.
- Assessment: diagram-labelling (body regions) plus matching (organ system → one movement-relevant function); retrieval-based.
- Safe self-application: learners may point to regions on their own body to reinforce vocabulary; no self-assessment of health or structure.
- YouTube mapping: strong visual/animation candidate.

**1.2 — Directional terms and planes of motion**
- Outcomes: use directional terms correctly; name the three cardinal planes with an everyday movement example for each; distinguish a plane of motion from a joint action (previewed; developed in Module 4).
- Assessment: labelling plus a "name the plane" exercise from a short movement clip.
- Safe self-application: learners may describe the plane of their own simple, pain-free movement in neutral language; not a form or injury-risk assessment.
- YouTube mapping: pairs with 1.1 in one video, or as a short follow-up.

**1.3 — The skeleton, joints and connective tissue**
- Outcomes: identify commonly referenced bones and joints at an introductory level; classify a joint by general type; describe the general role of ligaments, tendons and cartilage without claims about an individual's tissue health.
- Assessment: labelling (skeleton/joint diagram) plus matching (joint type → example joint).
- Safe self-application: no self-assessment of joint health, laxity or injury — naming and classifying only.
- YouTube mapping: strong visual/animation candidate; diagrams reusable in Term 2.

**1.4 — Major muscles and how they are named**
- Outcomes: name major exercise-relevant muscles at an introductory level; explain at least two muscle-naming conventions (location, shape, number of heads/origins, action); distinguish prime mover, synergist/contributor and stabiliser, ready for use in the Lat Pulldown Lab.
- Assessment: labelling plus matching (muscle → naming convention it illustrates); feeds directly into the Lat Pulldown Lab's toggles.
- Safe self-application: learners may observe their own muscles contracting during a simple, pain-free movement to reinforce naming; no assessment of imbalance, weakness or injury.
- YouTube mapping: strong candidate; natural lead-in video for the Lat Pulldown Lab.

## Module 2 — Physiology of Movement

Prerequisite: Module 1.

| # | Chapter | Prerequisites | Core vocabulary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2.1 | Nervous system: sensation, coordination and motor control | Module 1 | proprioception, motor unit, motor control, reflex, central/peripheral nervous system (introductory distinction only) |
| 2.2 | Muscle structure, contraction and fibre types | 1.4, 2.1 | skeletal muscle, fascicle, sliding filament concept, fibre type (Type I / Type II, qualitative only) |
| 2.3 | Cardiovascular and respiratory roles in oxygen transport and exchange | 2.1 | cardiac output (qualitative), ventilation, gas exchange, heart rate, acute cardiovascular response |
| 2.4 | Endocrine foundations, homeostasis and acute responses to activity | 2.1–2.3 | homeostasis, endocrine system, hormone, acute response (contrasted with chronic adaptation, previewed for Term 3) |

**2.1 — Nervous system: sensation, coordination and motor control**
- Outcomes: describe, in general terms, how the nervous system senses position (proprioception) and produces voluntary movement; explain the general idea of a motor unit's relevance to force at an introductory level, with no numeric claims; describe a reflex as fast, non-diagnostic nervous-system function.
- Assessment: concept-matching plus short "explain in your own words" prompts.
- Safe self-application: learners may notice their own balance/coordination during a simple, low-risk activity (for example briefly standing on one leg) in neutral language; any dizziness, numbness or loss of balance triggers the standing referral line, not a lesson activity.
- YouTube mapping: good animation candidate (nerve signal → muscle contraction pathway).

**2.2 — Muscle structure, contraction and fibre types**
- Outcomes: describe the general structure of skeletal muscle and the sliding-filament concept qualitatively; distinguish, at an introductory level, the general characteristics of slower, more fatigue-resistant fibres versus faster, more powerful fibres, without asserting an individual's fibre-type proportions; explain why fibre type is a population-level generalisation Fit360 cannot measure or claim for an individual learner.
- Assessment: true/false-with-explanation plus a "match the description to fibre type" exercise; no numeric or percentage-based questions.
- Safe self-application: no self-testing of "which fibre type am I" — population-level physiology only.
- YouTube mapping: animation candidate; script must state plainly that no percentages are being claimed for any individual.

**2.3 — Cardiovascular and respiratory roles in oxygen transport and exchange**
- Outcomes: trace, in general terms, the path of oxygen from breathing in to use in a working muscle; explain qualitatively why heart rate and breathing rate rise during activity; describe heart rate as an observable, non-diagnostic signal.
- Assessment: sequence/flow-diagram ordering exercise (order the steps of oxygen delivery) plus short-answer.
- Safe self-application: learners may notice their own heart rate or breathing rising during ordinary activity in neutral language; Fit360 never asks them to judge a specific number as healthy or unhealthy (excluded per the safety policy), and chest pain or severe breathlessness triggers the emergency-care line, not a lesson prompt.
- YouTube mapping: strong visual/animation candidate (oxygen pathway diagram).

**2.4 — Endocrine foundations, homeostasis and acute responses to activity**
- Outcomes: explain homeostasis using a movement-relevant example; name one or two hormones commonly discussed in exercise contexts (for example adrenaline/epinephrine, cortisol) and their general, qualitative role in an acute response, with no dosing, therapeutic or diagnostic claims; explain why Fit360 will never interpret an individual's hormone levels or test results.
- Assessment: short-answer plus a concept check that explicitly tests "what this is NOT."
- Safe self-application: no self-application activity touches hormones or test results — conceptual only; if a learner raises their own lab results, the standing referral line and the AI assistant rules in the [safety and AI policy](../product/safety-and-ai-policy.md) apply.
- YouTube mapping: lower priority for an early video; a candidate for a later "stress response to exercise" explainer.

## Module 3 — Energy Systems and Recovery

Prerequisite: Module 2.

| # | Chapter | Prerequisites | Core vocabulary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3.1 | ATP as the immediate energy currency | Module 2 | ATP, ADP, energy currency, resynthesis |
| 3.2 | The three energy pathways: phosphagen, glycolytic and oxidative | 3.1 | phosphagen (ATP-PC) system, glycolytic system, oxidative system, anaerobic, aerobic |
| 3.3 | How the systems overlap, and the influence of duration and intensity | 3.2 | energy-system overlap/continuum, intensity, duration |
| 3.4 | Recovery as a general physiological concept | 3.1–3.3, 0.3 | recovery, fatigue, overreaching (introductory mention only, no programming prescription) |

**3.1 — ATP as the immediate energy currency**
- Outcomes: explain why ATP is described as the body's immediate energy currency; describe qualitatively that ATP must be continually resynthesised during activity.
- Assessment: short concept check ("why can't the body just store enough ATP for a workout?").
- Safe self-application: none — pure concept chapter.
- YouTube mapping: short animation; strong opener for an energy-systems mini-series.

**3.2 — The three energy pathways: phosphagen, glycolytic and oxidative**
- Outcomes: name the three pathways and describe, qualitatively, the type of effort each suits (very short/high-intensity; short-to-moderate; longer/lower-intensity); state that specific duration cut-offs vary between sources and individuals, so Fit360 presents cited ranges rather than fixed numbers.
- Assessment: matching exercise (activity example → dominant pathway) using clearly qualitative, source-cited ranges only — no invented precise thresholds.
- Safe self-application: none; any numeric range used must trace to a cited source (for example an ACSM or NSCA position stand) and flag where evidence is mixed.
- YouTube mapping: strong video candidate — the classic "three energy systems" explainer, differentiated by citing sources.

**3.3 — How the systems overlap, and the influence of duration and intensity**
- Outcomes: explain that the three pathways operate together rather than as on/off switches; describe qualitatively how duration and intensity shift each pathway's relative contribution.
- Assessment: scenario exercise — given an activity description, describe the likely qualitative mix of pathways with reasoning.
- Safe self-application: none.
- YouTube mapping: can combine with 3.2 into one video, or stand as a short follow-up.

**3.4 — Recovery as a general physiological concept**
- Outcomes: describe recovery, generally, as the body restoring energy stores and repairing tissue after activity; distinguish general recovery education from individualised recovery prescription or rehabilitation, which Fit360 does not provide; identify signs that call for clinical input rather than "pushing through," reusing 0.3's referral rule.
- Assessment: short-answer plus a scenario ("continue training tomorrow" vs "this needs a clinician") reusing the 0.3 decision format.
- Safe self-application: learners may note their own general fatigue or soreness in neutral language; Fit360 gives no individualised recovery prescription, and concerning or persistent symptoms trigger the standing referral line.
- YouTube mapping: good finale video pairing with 3.1–3.3 as a mini-series.

## Module 4 — Movement Vocabulary and Introductory Biomechanics

Prerequisite: Modules 1–3 (this module reuses anatomy, physiology and the safety-language rule from 0.3).

| # | Chapter | Prerequisites | Core vocabulary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4.1 | Joint actions and planes of motion (applied) | 1.2, 1.3 | flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, internal/external rotation, pronation, supination |
| 4.2 | Force, torque and leverage | 4.1 | force, torque, lever, lever arm, moment arm (introductory mention) |
| 4.3 | Balance, centre of mass and stability | 4.1, 4.2 | centre of mass, base of support, stability, balance |
| 4.4 | Mobility, coordination and movement variability | 4.1–4.3 | mobility, flexibility, coordination, movement variability |
| 4.5 | Describing movement without labelling it normal, abnormal, safe or unsafe for a specific person | 0.3, 4.1–4.4 | descriptive (non-evaluative) language, movement assessment (contrasted, not taught) |
| 4.6 | Flagship lab: the Lat Pulldown Lab | 1.4, 4.1, 4.2, 4.5 | see the [lab specification](labs/lat-pulldown-lab.md) |

**4.1 — Joint actions and planes of motion (applied)**
- Outcomes: name and demonstrate (verbally/visually) the major joint actions at an introductory level; connect each joint action to the plane of motion from 1.2.
- Assessment: label/match exercise (movement clip or diagram → joint action + plane); directly reused by the Lat Pulldown Lab.
- Safe self-application: learners may name the joint action of their own simple, pain-free movement; no form correction or injury-risk judgement.
- YouTube mapping: strong candidate; a visual joint-action reference video reusable across Term 2.

**4.2 — Force, torque and leverage**
- Outcomes: define force, torque and leverage in plain language with a movement example each; explain qualitatively how changing a lever arm changes the torque needed at a joint, without prescribing "better" or "worse" technique for an individual.
- Assessment: applied short-answer/diagram exercise (identify the lever arm in a simple diagram) — conceptual, not a technique-correction exercise.
- Safe self-application: none beyond the general movement-description boundary; no individual technique verdicts.
- YouTube mapping: strong candidate — anchors the Lat Pulldown Lab's "why does grip width feel different" content.

**4.3 — Balance, centre of mass and stability**
- Outcomes: define centre of mass and base of support and their general relationship to balance; describe qualitatively what affects stability in a simple stance or movement.
- Assessment: short-answer/diagram exercise (predict, in general terms, which of two stances is more stable and why).
- Safe self-application: learners may notice their own balance in a simple, low-risk stance; any fall risk, dizziness or instability concern triggers the standing referral line, not a balance "test."
- YouTube mapping: can combine with 4.2 or stand alone as a short video.

**4.4 — Mobility, coordination and movement variability**
- Outcomes: distinguish mobility from flexibility and from stability at an introductory level; explain movement variability as a normal, general concept without labelling any individual's variation as a fault.
- Assessment: short-answer plus a concept check distinguishing the three terms.
- Safe self-application: learners may observe that their own version of a simple movement differs from someone else's, in neutral language; Fit360 does not rate this good/bad/normal/abnormal.
- YouTube mapping: useful short explainer; also a bridge to Term 2 (technique).

**4.5 — Describing movement without labelling it normal, abnormal, safe or unsafe for a specific person**
- Outcomes: explain why Fit360 avoids labelling an individual's movement as normal, abnormal, safe or unsafe; practise describing a movement using only 4.1–4.4's vocabulary rather than a value judgement; restate the standing referral rule in this specific context.
- Assessment: rewrite exercise — convert an evaluative statement (for example "that squat looks unsafe") into a descriptive one using taught vocabulary. This is the direct safety-language rehearsal for the Lat Pulldown Lab.
- Safe self-application: this chapter is itself a safety-boundary chapter; it sets the language rule the Lat Pulldown Lab and all of Term 2 must follow.
- YouTube mapping: optional; likely more valuable as an in-app/text chapter than a video, since it is boundary-setting rather than visually rich.

**4.6 — Flagship lab: the Lat Pulldown Lab**
- Outcomes, interaction states, explanations, quiz approach, source requirements, safety wording and exclusions are specified in full in [labs/lat-pulldown-lab.md](labs/lat-pulldown-lab.md), not duplicated here.
- YouTube mapping: flagship first-lab video; strong candidate alongside Module 0 for the very first videos published.

## Safe self-application

Activities may ask learners to observe and describe their own movement in neutral language, for example by naming the plane of a simple motion or noticing changes in breathing during ordinary activity. They must not ask learners to self-diagnose, test through pain, continue through concerning symptoms or use lessons as rehabilitation. Chapter 0.3 teaches this rule explicitly; every later chapter's "safe self-application" line above is a specific application of it, never an exception to it.

Standing guidance: *stop activity and seek qualified care for concerning symptoms, injury or health questions. Fit360 education is not a substitute for medical assessment, treatment, rehabilitation or therapeutic nutrition advice.*

## Evidence and assessment

Learning checks focus on retrieval, matching terms, labelling diagrams, explaining how systems interact and interpreting general claims with evidence in mind. They never assess diagnosis, treatment selection, rehabilitation planning or individual nutrition therapy. Per-chapter assessment approaches are listed above; all of them are formative and ungraded at this architecture stage — whether any chapter later becomes a graded/gating check (for example before unlocking Term 2) is a product decision, not a curriculum one, and is not made here.

Numeric claims (durations, ranges, thresholds) appear only where a chapter table above says so, and only when traced to a cited source at drafting time. No chapter in this document states an invented number, percentage or study result; where a range is implied (for example energy-system duration cut-offs), the chapter note above says explicitly that it must be sourced and may vary.
